Handing you the brush

On color schemes, why they're going away, and what comes next.

Every color scheme in Baseline was a decision I made for you.

Nord. Gruvbox. Catppuccin. Rosé Pine. I picked each one, hand-tuned the values, shipped it, and quietly committed to maintaining it forever. The list grew. And with it, a problem I hadn't anticipated: the more schemes I added, the less any of them felt like yours.

Picking a name from a dropdown isn't customizing your theme. It's adopting someone else's taste and hoping it fits.

What's new

Instead of a list of schemes, you now get three controls: Spectrum picks your color, Vibrancy sets how saturated it feels, and Clarity shapes the overall contrast.

From those three values, Baseline builds your entire palette — backgrounds, tints, borders, text — using OKLCH, a color model that matches how humans actually perceive color. The results are cohesive in a way that's genuinely hard to achieve by hand.

The Signature shapes the underlying palette structure — how contrast is distributed, how much color bleeds into neutral surfaces. Think of it as the character of the theme, and the three sliders as your voice within it.

The community part

The old schemes haven't disappeared. They've moved to Baseline Marketplace, both as converted dynamic versions and as their original static builds.

But more importantly: so can yours.

The Marketplace was always meant to be a community space. That part was harder to believe when the color section was a fixed list I personally maintained. Now it isn't. Anyone can submit a palette. If you land on something you love, you can share it — and someone else can load it in one click, then adjust the Spectrum to make it their own.

The schemes I shipped were a ceiling. This is an open floor.

One honest limitation

The dynamic system derives everything from a single hue, which means it produces cohesive, monochromatic-leaning results. If you want a multi-color setup — say, a warm background with a cool accent — that's not something the sliders can do. For that, the static presets on Marketplace are still the right tool.

Your existing Style Settings config keeps working. The old variables are preserved as a compatibility layer until the next major version.

Everything else is better. And for the first time, it's actually yours.
